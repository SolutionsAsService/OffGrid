# RV Road Trip, Repair & Cooling-Season Migration Plan

> **Route objective:** Move from Taos, New Mexico toward Southern California while keeping the RV, the dogs, and remote-work capability functional — using the trip itself to progressively repair and upgrade the RV — then move into Phoenix only after temperatures become reasonable.

---

## 1. Mission

The objective is **not** to drive straight from Taos to Los Angeles.

The objective is to:

1. Leave Taos safely.
2. Use inexpensive/free public-land camping wherever practical.
3. Keep the RV at higher elevations while temperatures are manageable.
4. Repair the RV progressively instead of trying to fix everything at once.
5. Maintain reliable internet for work.
6. Keep five dogs comfortable.
7. Avoid low-desert heat while the RV has no A/C.
8. Reach Los Angeles without arriving with an unfinished/unsafe RV.
9. Eventually move toward Phoenix when temperatures drop enough to make the desert manageable.

### Core strategy

```text
TAOS
  │
  ▼
GALLUP
  │
  │  Initial inspection / staging
  ▼
WILLIAMS
  │
  │  RV repairs + systems work
  ▼

  │

  │  Work + repairs + testing
  ▼
SOUTHERN CALIFORNIA
  │
  ▼
PHOENIX
  │
  │  Only after temperatures become reasonable
  ▼
LONGER-TERM DESTINATION
```

The exact order between Los Angeles and Phoenix can change depending on weather, RV condition, work requirements, and repair progress.

# 2. Why We Are NOT Going Directly to Phoenix

Phoenix is currently the wrong environment for an RV without A/C.

Phoenix normally remains extremely hot through September. Current September 2026 observations have already reached approximately **104°F**, and the September climate normal is around **100°F for the average daily maximum**.

October is substantially better, with a normal high around **90°F**.

Therefore:

> **Phoenix is a destination for later in the season, not the immediate destination.**

The RV should first spend time at elevation.

### Critical issue

The previously melted/blown fuse situation means the high-current electrical system deserves priority.

**Do not simply install a larger fuse.**

A melted fuse holder can indicate:

* Excessive current
* Undersized wire
* Loose connection
* Bad crimp
* Poor-quality fuse holder
* Corrosion
* Excessive resistance
* Incorrect fuse selection
* Inverter overload

The damaged section should be repaired before relying on the system for major loads.

# 11. Solar Repair Rule

The system should be designed from the actual equipment specifications.

Before changing the wiring, document:

* Every solar panel label
* Panel Voc
* Panel Vmp
* Panel Isc
* Panel Imp
* Number of panels
* Battery voltage
* Battery chemistry
* Battery Ah
* Battery configuration
* MPPT model
* MPPT maximum PV voltage
* MPPT maximum charging current
* Inverter model
* Inverter continuous wattage
* Inverter surge wattage
* Existing wire gauges
* Existing fuse ratings

### Do not guess.

The final configuration should be calculated from those numbers.

# 17. Do Not Try to Finish Everything Immediately

Use the:

> **Repair → Test → Drive → Repair → Test**

cycle.

For example:

```text
Repair electrical system
        ↓
Run system overnight
        ↓
Monitor voltage/current/temperature
        ↓
Drive 50–100 miles
        ↓
Inspect connections
        ↓
Continue
```

This is safer than performing twenty modifications and discovering one major failure after leaving town.

# 23. Phoenix Strategy

Phoenix is deliberately postponed.

## Trigger-based entry

Do not choose Phoenix based solely on the calendar.

Choose it based on:

1. Forecast
2. Overnight temperatures
3. Daytime temperatures
4. RV A/C status
5. Dog safety
6. Ability to obtain shade
7. Ability to obtain electrical power

### Preferred threshold

As a planning target:

> **Wait for sustained daytime highs around 90–95°F or lower, with acceptable overnight temperatures.**

This is not a hard safety threshold; the RV's actual interior temperature matters more than the outdoor forecast.

October becomes substantially more attractive because Phoenix's normal October high is around **90°F**, versus roughly **101°F in September**.

# 34. The Most Important Rule

The trip should be treated as a **mobile RV rehabilitation project**, not a vacation itinerary.

> **Move → stabilize → repair → test → work → move → repair → test.**

Not:

> **Drive as far as possible → discover a problem → become stranded.**
